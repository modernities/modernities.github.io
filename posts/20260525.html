Instant

Where the median falls inside the working life of its own labor force, ancestor and descendant occupy a single cohort, and the inherited has nothing to inherit.

A city’s age is the integral of the ages of its residents weighted by their probability of remaining. By this metric most metropoles are older than they appear. One is markedly younger. Youth advertises itself by absence — by the absence of the institutions that require old people in order to function: the parish, the inheritance, the funeral that seats six generations at one table.

The classical city is built by its dead. Walls keep enemies out and ancestors in. The cemetery is downtown. The cathedral is downtown. The foundation stone bears a name no living person bears, and the name confers right of residence on those who do. This is the unmarked default arrangement. To depart from it requires an active disorganization of the temporal stack.

Disorganization can be induced by velocity. Recruit a population at a rate exceeding the local replacement rate, and the median age detaches from the local biological clock. The city refreshes. Average tenure of a resident: under seven years. Average distance from birthplace: nine hundred kilometers. Average grandparent: in a different province, on a different dialect, at a phone call that does not always connect.

What disappears under these conditions is the social institution of memory. Individual recollections abound. The bodies who would gather to compare them are absent by design. Recollection persists as a private operation; the public technology of memory — the table, the calendar feast, the corridor lined with photographs of the previously employed — has no plausible host.

The substitution of the local tongue followed the same logic. Cantonese is the native language of the territory. Mandarin is the functional language of the city. The migrants carried it in by population physics; the prior tongue receded to the wet markets, the older temples, the relatives on speakerphone. A language can be displaced without any agent deciding to displace it. One need only outnumber it for a generation.

The shorthand: a metropolis of twenty million whose median resident has not yet seen forty; whose oldest hospital is younger than its mayor; whose first university opened in the same decade as its first subway; whose principal export, after electronics, is the documentary record of itself in motion. Adjoining, across an administrative seam, a territory of seven million whose median sees sixty, whose hospitals predate aviation, and whose principal export, increasingly, is the population that remembers what the territory was for.

The seam is the only stable referent in the region. North of it the median falls each year. South of it the median climbs. The gradient between the two ages is the system’s only reliable instrument, and the instrument is asymmetrical: the young side can read the old side; the old side has progressively less capacity to read back.

A city that refreshes faster than it ages requires only a strategy for the next intake.
